# Domain — *Chrysoperla carnea*

Green lacewing. Cross-agent tables: `predators-comparison.md`.
Pest and program: `aphids.md`.


## Model


### The stage decides the product

Sold at three stages, and they are effectively three different
products.

| | Eggs on jute cord | Larvae | Adults |
|---|---|---|---|
| Onset | 3–5 d | **same day** | never |
| Predatory | after hatch | **yes** | **no** — pollen and nectar |
| Price | $38 / 2 000 → **$0.019** | $89 / 500 → **$0.178** | $86 / 100 |
| Rate, preventive | 20–50 /m² | 5–10 /m² | — |
| Rate, light to severe | 50–100 /m² | 20–50 /m² | — |
| Dose verifiable | **no** | **yes** | — |

Other formats: 1 000 eggs $38 · 10 000 eggs $120 · 1 000 larvae
$158 · 10 000 larvae $492 · 1 000 adults $435.

- **never buy adults for aphid control.** They take pollen and nectar and disperse

- label cadence for both usable formats: **every 2 weeks**


### The 4:1 ratio is attrition, already priced in

Twenty eggs are recommended where five larvae are.

- consequence: hatch failure and cannibalism at emergence are **already inside the recommended number**

- consequence: the "unknown dose" objection to the cord is answered by the label rather than by faith — you cannot observe the hatch, but the supplier has corrected for it

- eggs are ~10× cheaper per individual than larvae, and after the 4:1 correction still **~2.3× cheaper per effective larva**


### Cannibalism runs through everything

Larvae eat unhatched eggs, each other, and other beneficials.

- consequence: **spreading cords is a requirement, not advice.** Bunched cords lose most of the cohort at emergence

- consequence: **weekly egg application is partly self-defeating** — last week's larvae are patrolling the bed when this week's cord goes up, and a share of the new eggs is eaten before hatching

- consequence: the discount grows the **cleaner** the crop is, because hungry larvae with no aphids turn on whatever is available

- mitigation: hang each week's cords in a different part of the bed from the previous week's

- larvae **cannibalise in transit** if the buckwheat carrier settles → dead-on-arrival is real and invisible

- **documented to damage co-released beneficials.** A compatibility trial reported *Nabis* "moderating the negative effects of chrysopes" on a parasitoid — a plain statement that chrysopes harm agents released alongside them

- consequence: never pair a lacewing line with another beneficial on the same crop
  → `predators-comparison.md § Combinations`


### Failure modes

- **larvae cannot search.** A larva walks. Placed 2 m from a colony it may never find it → placement precision does the work flight would do for another agent

- too large to work a lettuce heart

- shelf life of days; cannot be stockpiled against a future outbreak

- **the jute cord is a foreign object in a crop that gets cut.** Jute in a lettuce bed goes to the wash line with the leaves → hang on a wire above the canopy or at the bed edge, and count them out and back in

- as a **curative** the order-to-delivery lag matters: a colony found after the Thursday cut-off waits ~10 days, and larvae cannot be held


### Roles

**Cord — the cheap standing line.** Cheapest predation available by
a wide margin. Its cost is an unverifiable dose and a physical
object in the crop.

- one 2 000-egg cord over 46.5 m² = **43 eggs/m²**, the top of the preventive band, from a single pack with no fractional ordering

**Larvae — the curative, and the most predictable line.** You see and
count what you bought; nothing depends on a hatch you cannot check.

- 500 larvae covers ~50 m² of hot spot at 10 /m²

- most robust of the realistic options against a bad week
  → `predators-comparison.md § Fragility`


### Verdict

The cord is the cheapest predation on the list and the least
verifiable. Larvae are the most predictable option and the natural
curative. Cannibalism is the thread connecting every weakness of
both.
