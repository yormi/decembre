# Domain — *Nabis*

Damsel bug. Cross-agent tables: `predators-comparison.md`.
Pest and program: `aphids.md`.


## Model


### What arrives

**Supplied as eggs.** Both nymphs and adults are predatory.

| | |
|---|---|
| Onset | **2–3 d** — nymphs feed from hatching |
| Development | 28 d egg to adult |
| Adult life | ~5 weeks |
| Working life per individual | **~9 weeks** |
| Feeding stages | nymph **and** adult |
| Diet | true generalist — survives lean weeks better than any other agent |
| Cold tolerance | high |
| Price | ~$100 / 300 eggs → **$0.33** — an indicative target, not a confirmed listing |

- fastest onset of any agent shipped as eggs, and second only to the same-day larval products

- **cheapest per unit of standing predation time** — one individual works ~9 weeks


### The longevity is wasted on a cut crop

- nymph to adult is **28 days**; a bed is cut at **14**

- consequence: the nymph never matures, never reproduces, and never becomes the long-lived adult the price reflects

- consequence: **you buy a nine-week animal and use fourteen days of it**

- consequence: *Nabis* is priced for a long-season crop. On a cut crop that pricing is simply wrong for the buyer — **worst cost per predator-day of the realistic options**, $0.030 against $0.0019 for a lacewing egg

- its one real edge over the cord is **speed**: day 2–3 against day 3–5. One or two days on a fourteen-day window, bought at 16× the price per predator-day


### Intraguild predation

A generalist true bug eats parasitoid mummies and other predators'
larvae.

**Trial evidence.** *Aphidius* + *Chrysoperla* + *Nabis* against
*Aphidius* + *Chrysoperla*:

- mummy counts were **not** reduced by adding *Nabis*

- they were **higher** with *Nabis* present — the bug ate chrysopes, and the chrysopes had been eating mummies

- the trialist's read: little chance *Nabis* reduces the efficacy of other aphid biocontrols

How much that settles:

- the result is **mechanistically coherent** rather than a bare null, which raises its weight — it names *why*, and the why checks out

- **but it protected a parasitoid, and this program has none**

- **the mechanism confirms the concern rather than dissolving it.** *Nabis* helped the parasitoid precisely **by predating chrysopes** — the same predation is a loss wherever a lacewing line is paid for

- consequence: strong evidence it is safe alongside **parasitoids**; direct evidence it **suppresses *Chrysoperla***; **no evidence either way on *Aphidoletes***

- consequence: never put *Nabis* on the same crop as a lacewing line

- neutrality holds **only if it is the sole agent** — which it is not while *Aphidoletes* runs on tomato and adults fly between bays

- the one question that could still change a tomato-only deployment: **does *Nabis* attack *Aphidoletes* larvae?**


### Failure modes

- **commercial availability is not established.** The price is an indicative target for a product not confirmed purchasable on a weekly subscription. A standing line cannot rest on that

- **the shipping substrate is unknown.** *Nabis* lay into plant tissue; if eggs arrive on plant material, that material enters a crop that gets cut — the same foreign-object problem as the jute cord. Ask before ordering

- as a generalist it survives clean weeks well, which is a strength for persistence and a liability for everything else on the leaf


### Verdict

Best gap-filler on paper. Priced for a long-season crop, not
confirmed buyable, and it eats the cheapest agent on the list. Only
viable as a **sole** agent, or spatially separated onto a crop that
carries no lacewing.
