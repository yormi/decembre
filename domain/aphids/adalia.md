# Domain — *Adalia bipunctata*

Two-spot ladybird. Cross-agent tables: `predators-comparison.md`.
Pest and program: `aphids.md`.


## Model


### What arrives

**Supplied as larvae.** The only agent whose juveniles **cannot fly
away** from the bed they were placed on.

| | |
|---|---|
| Onset | **same day** |
| Feeding stages | larvae; adults disperse |
| Diet | generalist aphid predator |
| Price | Koppert Aphidalia 100 larvae **$54.62** → **$0.546** |
| | NIC $69.99, count unstated, listed out of stock |
| Lead time | **10–14 days, pre-order** (Koppert) |
| Shelf life | 1–2 days |


### Why it is out

- **10× the unit price of *Chrysoperla* larvae** for the same same-day action → at 5 /m² over 93 m² it costs ~$254/week; even a token 1 /m² is ~$55/week, more than the whole *Aphidoletes* backbone for a fifth of the area

- the **10–14 day lead time** disqualifies it as a **curative** — a colony found today cannot be answered in a fortnight

- it does **not** disqualify a standing line: on a subscription the lead time is a one-time offset, then boxes arrive weekly like any other

- consequence: ***Adalia* is out on price alone**, not on lead time and not on biology. The larvae-cannot-fly advantage never gets a chance to pay


### Operational trap

**Koppert ships orders in full.** A pre-order line added to a weekly
subscription holds the whole box until the slow item is ready.

- consequence: **never mix a lead-time item into a standing order.** Place it separately or not at all


### Not the same as bulk "ladybugs"

*Hippodamia convergens*, the cheap bulk product, is a different
proposition entirely.

- **field-collected** from overwintering aggregations, sold as adults still in diapause

- they **disperse within days** of release and may carry parasites

- consequence: cheap per insect and near-worthless per insect delivered. Not a substitute for reared *Adalia* larvae


### Verdict

Out on price. Revisit only if a supplier quotes larvae near
lacewing-larva pricing.
