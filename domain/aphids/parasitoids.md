# Domain — parasitoids

*Aphidius*, *Aphelinus*. Excluded from this program on a single
mechanism. Cross-agent tables: `predators-comparison.md`.


## Model


### The mummy is the whole problem

A parasitoid larva spins silk beneath the aphid and **cements the
hardened cuticle to the leaf** before pupating inside. The mummy is
an anchored, weatherproof case built to survive rain and wind.

- consequence: a bubbler is gentler than rain. **Mummies are the only residue class the wash line fails on**

- consequence: on a washed cut-leaf crop, **every mummy produced is a defect the process cannot fix**

- consequence: predators are preferable as a **category**, not marginally
  → `aphids.md § What survives a bubbler wash`

**Spatial separation does not work.** Parasitoid adults fly.

- releasing them only on an unwashed crop still seeds mummies in the washed one

- consequence: to eliminate mummies, **do not release parasitoids in the house at all**

**But Décembre's own history softens the rule.** *A. colemani*
mummies have never been a bubbler problem here.

- attachment is a property of the mummy; **reaching the pack is a property of density** and of which leaves get cut

- low parasitism, or mummies forming mostly on older leaves trimmed at harvest, both produce "no mummy problem" without contradicting the mechanism

- consequence: the rule is strong where mummy density is high and weak where it is low → **observed pack-table history outranks the mechanism alone**


### What parasitoids are actually good at

The reason they keep coming back into the discussion.

- **they find low-density colonies better than any predator.** That is the one capability a predator-only program lacks, and it is exactly what fails on a 14-day bed

- they leave a **visible working signal** — mummies double as a diagnostic, telling you the agent is matching the species

- consequence: if a crop ever becomes genuinely clean for stretches, a low-density finder is the tool that fits, and the mummy objection weakens in the same conditions that make it needed
  → `aphids.md § Population suppression vs a clean pack`


### *Aphidius colemani* — the narrowest agent in the set

| | |
|---|---|
| Hosts | *Myzus persicae*, *Aphis gossypii* — effectively nothing else |
| **Not** hosts | *Nasonovia ribisnigri*, *Macrosiphum euphorbiae*, *Aulacorthum solani* |
| Price | ~$0.06 / mummy (assumed) |

- consequence: its value is **binary on species identity**. Against *Myzus* it is one of the best low-density finders available and very cheap; against *Nasonovia* it does nothing at all

- consequence: buying it blind in a program with no species identification is a coin flip

- **the release history is its own diagnostic**, and it costs one pack:
  - mummies appear → the dominant species is a host → the line is worth buying
  - **no mummies after 6 weeks** (two generations) → no host present → drop it, never re-order

- consequence: adding it back **cannot reduce coverage** — it either adds control against *Myzus* or does nothing. Its risks are mummies and wasted money, not a gap

- activity falls off below ~15 °C


### *Aphelinus abdominalis*

- cool-tolerant, targets **large-bodied aphids** — *Macrosiphum*, *Aulacorthum*

- **host-feeds**: kills aphids it does not parasitize, adding kill without adding a mummy

- year-round agent, not a winter-only complement — the seasonal on/off switch once written for it was unjustified

- same mummy objection


### Banker plants

Cereal + *Rhopalosiphum padi*. The classic parasitoid support
system.

- sustains *Aphidius colemani* between outbreaks; the banker aphid **cannot feed on lettuce or tomato**, so the colony is safe to keep

- a banker **substitutes for release frequency** when the crop carries prey, and becomes the only place an agent can feed and lay when the crop is clean

- **but carrying capacity must match the pressure released against it.** A cereal tray carries 1 000–5 000 aphids; a 1 000-pupae predator release demands hundreds of thousands

- consequence: banker systems pair with **low-rate parasitoid programs**, where the wasp population self-regulates against prey supply. They cannot absorb predator flooding
  → `predators-comparison.md § Combinations`


### Verdict

Excluded by default on the mummy mechanism. The exclusion is
strongest on the washed crop and weakest at low parasitism, which is
also where their low-density finding would be most valuable — a real
tension, not a settled one.
